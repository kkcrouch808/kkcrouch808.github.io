## Overview

### The problem
Finding a roommate shouldn’t feel like gambling—but for many UH Mānoa students, it does. Every semester, hundreds of students search for someone compatible to live with in dorms like Hale Aloha, Gateway, or Frear, or in off-campus apartments around Mānoa and Moʻiliʻili. This challenge is especially daunting for students who arrive without a local support network; depending on the year and dataset, 31% to 36% of UH Mānoa students come from out of state, meaning nearly one-third of the campus navigates Hawaiʻi housing with limited connections.

Because UH lacks a centralized, student-friendly matching system, most students resort to improvised methods—scrolling through Snapchat stories, Instagram posts, Discord servers, or even dating apps—to find someone with similar habits. These platforms weren’t designed for housing compatibility, and students often discover too late that their roommate’s lifestyle, values, or schedule simply does not align with their own.

The result? Conflicts over noise, cleanliness, guests, privacy, or sleep schedules; mid-semester housing changes; or the dreaded “I can’t live here anymore” moment.

### The solution
**Mānoa RoomieMatch** aims to bring clarity, personalization, and peace of mind to the housing search. Our platform allows UH students to create a lifestyle profile that captures the day-to-day factors that truly determine roommate compatibility: sleep routines, study habits, noise tolerance, cooking frequency, cleanliness levels, social preferences, personality traits, and housing goals.

The “special sauce” is an AI-powered compatibility engine that compares two students’ profiles and generates:

a compatibility score from 0 to 100

a detailed narrative explaining points of harmony and tension

suggestions for managing potential conflicts

optional AI-generated templates for messaging a potential roommate

Rather than relying on luck or vague impressions from social media, students receive clear, personalized, and practical insight into whether a roommate pairing is likely to work—before signing a lease or dorm agreement.

## Approach

The approach behind Mānoa RoomieMatch is to make the roommate search easier, clearer, and less stressful for UH students. After a student creates their lifestyle and housing profile, the system saves their information and uses it to find matches. The goal is to help students understand who they might be compatible with before they decide to live together.

Students start by logging in with their UH email. From there, they complete a lifestyle questionnaire that covers things like sleep schedule, cleanliness, study habits, noise levels, and housing preferences. Once their profile is finished, the student can browse potential roommates. They can filter by different traits, such as dorm preference, budget, personality style, or similar interests.

AI plays a major role in helping students understand their matches. When the system compares two profiles, the AI creates a compatibility score and explains why the two students might work well together—or why they might not. It also suggests conversation starters so students can reach out comfortably. As an optional feature, students can choose to get notifications when someone new joins the platform with similar preferences.

Administrators also have tools to keep the system safe and up to date. They can review flagged profiles, create new lifestyle categories, adjust the questionnaire, and make sure everything on the site stays appropriate for students.

## Some mockup page ideas

The platform includes several pages that help students move through the system smoothly.

The Landing Page introduces what RoomieMatch does and includes a simple button that encourages students to get started. It also shows a short explanation of how the AI makes matching easier.

The User Home Page is where students land after they sign in. This page shows their main profile information, their current match suggestions, and any notifications they may have.

The Profile Setup Page guides students through a step-by-step survey that asks about daily habits and lifestyle choices. A progress bar shows how much is left, and students can save their answers as they go.

On the Browse Matches Page, students can scroll through roommate options. Each match shows a percentage score, a short description, and a button to view more details.

The Match Details Page compares two profiles side by side and includes an AI-written explanation of the match. It also gives suggestions for how the two students can start a conversation.

Finally, the Admin Home Page allows administrators to check flagged content, update lifestyle categories, and manage user accounts.

## Use case ideas

There are many ways students and administrators might interact with the platform. For example, a new student could visit the site, read about how RoomieMatch works, and log in with their UH email. After completing the lifestyle survey, they are taken to their home page, where they see recommended matches and learn how the scoring system works.

Another student who already knows what type of roommate they want can go directly to the Browse Matches page. They can filter by dorm choice, budget, or lifestyle preferences. After finding someone with a high match score, they can read the AI explanation and use the suggested message template to reach out.

Administrators use the system differently. They sign in and go to the Admin Home Page, where they can review any flagged content, update categories in the survey, and manage or remove user accounts if needed.

## Beyond the basics

There are several advanced features that could be added once the main system works well. One idea is a Dorm Recommendation System where the AI suggests UH dorms based on a student’s noise tolerance, room type preference, budget, or whether they want AC.

Another idea is Advanced Matching Insights, which could show students a visual breakdown of which lifestyle areas match well and which areas might cause conflict. A Roommate Agreement Generator could help roommates create a simple contract that outlines chores, quiet hours, guest rules, and shared responsibilities.

The system could also include a Schedule Compatibility Sync, where students connect their UH class schedules to see overlapping free time or morning and night routines. Finally, a Household Planner could help roommates keep track of chores, shared expenses, and grocery lists.
